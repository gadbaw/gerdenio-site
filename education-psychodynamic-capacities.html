The Psychodynamic Capacities: Making Depth Therapy Teachable

Max Levine, Psy.D.

Wednesday, August 26, 2026 · 12:15–1:45 PM Pacific

By invitationLive on Zoom90 minutes

Max Levine, Psy.D. presents the teaching model he has been developing for making relational psychodynamic therapy concrete and learnable: twelve psychodynamic capacities, the skills that cultivate them, and their translation into motivational-interviewing-compatible ambivalence formulations.

He'll also preview his current work on relational stances: crafting the therapy relationship itself to meet a patient's particular capacities. Ninety minutes, with time for discussion. Attendance is by invitation and free of charge.

Learning objectives

After this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Describe the twelve psychodynamic capacities and their role in a relational model of therapeutic change.
  2. Identify concrete, practicable skills that cultivate each capacity in psychotherapy.
  3. Formulate a patient's ambivalence as a two-sided decisional balance, with one side sustaining the status quo and the other supporting change, in a form compatible with motivational interviewing.
  4. Select the capacity most relevant to a particular patient and track movement from "sustain" toward "change" over the course of treatment.
  5. Explain how relational stances tailor the therapy relationship itself to a patient's particular capacity deficits.
